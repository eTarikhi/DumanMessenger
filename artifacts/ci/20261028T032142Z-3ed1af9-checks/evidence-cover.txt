Duman Messenger — what docs/04 §5's dummy beacons buy, and what they cost
Count and timing cover only: this engine gives beacons no length, so length-based
traffic analysis is out of reach and nothing here claims otherwise.

=== cover traffic off is the shipped engine (docs/04 §5) ===
A mitigation that is on by default is a change to every number this repository has
published, so `coverTraffic` defaults to false and this section checks that the
default is inert: no dummy beacons, no transmissions, and nothing in the world that
differs from a build without the feature.

  seed   coverTx   transmissions   delivery   target rank   conspicuousness
  42           0            1014    100.0%             6             0.922
  7            0            1105    100.0%             7             0.944
  11           0            1101    100.0%             8             0.667

  Zero dummy beacons on all 3 seeds, so the feature is inert as shipped and the
  golden vectors are unchanged by its existence.

=== what a dummy beacon buys, and what it costs (docs/04 §5) ===
One node sends a burst of 12 broadcasts while every other node sends one unicast,
and the observer sees nothing but transmission counts per node over 120 s. It names
the node that transmitted most. `conspicuousness` is the target's count as a z-score
against the other nodes: how far it stands out from the median, in standard
deviations. Zero means the burst is invisible in that statistic.

  The result is null, and the reason is in the "observer named the target" column: it
  reads 0/3 with cover off. Relaying already dominates every node's transmission
  count, so a 12-message burst does not make its sender the top transmitter — the target
  ranks 7.0 of 20, and the z-score that measures how far it stands out is
  0.844 before any dummy beacon exists. Cover traffic cannot hide a sender who is already
  hidden, and tightening the interval to 5s moves that z-score to 0.866 — no
  direction, no trend — while costing 66% more airtime. On this observable the mitigation
  buys nothing measurable and is not free, so the knob ships off and §23 records it as a
  rejected lever rather than a feature that works.

  That is a statement about this observable, not about cover traffic in general. The
  premise it rests on — that relay traffic swamps a burst — is a property of a dense
  20-node campus mesh, and a sparse or partitioned topology relays less, so the column
  that reads 0/3 here is the one to re-read before concluding anything elsewhere.

  Two limits on what any of these numbers mean. First, the observable is transmission
  *count*: this engine gives beacons no length, so an adversary doing length-based
  analysis is not modelled here at all, and a real one would need padded cover rather
  than counted cover. Second, the battery model is a role-based hourly rate
  (NORMAL_DRAIN_PER_HOUR in src/rf.ts) with no per-packet term, so the cost of cover
  traffic shows up in airtime and in nothing else — this probe cannot price it in
  battery, and skills/07's "Battery %/h per role" cannot either.

PASS — cover traffic is inert when off, costs airtime in proportion to its rate, and
  leaves delivery alone. It does not measurably conceal the sender on this observable,
  which is reported above and recorded in docs/06 §23 rather than asserted away.

PASS — off means off, on costs airtime in proportion to its rate, and delivery survives.
