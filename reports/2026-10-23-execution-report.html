1. What this repository is

A research and specification corpus - roughly 1,150 lines of Markdown across docs/01-05, roadmap/ and skills/00-08 - describing Duman Messenger: a serverless, offline-first messenger whose phones form the network. Android-first, Flutter with Kotlin/Swift transport bridges, a BATMAN-lite control plane with MPR flood suppression and Spray-and-Wait DTN store-carry-forward, Noise_XX end-to-end encryption, BLE for universality and Wi-Fi Direct for bandwidth. No servers, no internet, no accounts.

What it also contains now is the thing the roadmap asks for in Phase 2 and that every claim in the specification needed in order to be checked: a deterministic discrete-event mesh simulator in two languages, a React visualiser, a CI pipeline that fails when the two engines disagree, and the measurements that turn the specification's assertions into numbers with error bars.

2. The two engines and the determinism contract

Authoritative: Dart

sim/mesh_sim/ - the engine the roadmap names (mesh_sim.dart), headless, with a CLI that runs scenarios, asserts the acceptance criteria and exits non-zero when they fail, and carries probes for SOS reach, group delivery modes, media transport, and history repair.

Reference and visualiser: TypeScript

src/ - the same protocol as a React dashboard: a canvas world, a node inspector, a chat panel with group and media controls, a metrics dashboard, per-hop message traces and a pcap-like capture log viewer.

They are held together by test-fixtures/golden-vectors.json: 277 lines of exact integers and floats - relay transmissions, OGM counts, retry counts, hop sums, average score to six decimals, p95 latency to three - generated from the TypeScript engine by npm run golden and asserted by both. Three RNG fixed points are pinned in both languages. Every feature added in this session was required to leave that file byte-identical unless it changed behaviour, and each one that did was regenerated deterministically rather than patched by hand.

The contract is also the discipline: a field may be added to a message, a log line or a hop record without touching the vectors, because none of them draws randomness. A new behaviour moves them, and then the move has to be explained.

Receiver contention: where the margin in the acceptance gates went

Half-duplex gave a device one transmitter and left its receiver infinitely attentive. channelCapture: "first" closes that — the receiver locks onto whichever packet arrived first, and a transmitting node hears nothing — and because it is independent of the transmitter flag, the two mechanisms separate. The receiver is where contention lives: a transmit queue alone costs text 2.8 points of delivery, receiver collisions alone cost 13.6. Once collisions exist the queue earns its keep and is worth 4.9 points back, because serialising a node's own transmissions removes the overlaps it was causing everybody else — the closest thing to a CSMA result here. Collisions are paid in latency before delivery (quiet-mesh p95 4,472 → 7,534 ms for 1.1 points), because the ACK timeout re-sends into an idle radio: two packets sent to one receiver at the same instant produce one collision and two deliveries, which a test pins. Carrier sense: the mechanism real radios have, and why it is the pessimistic arm

channelAccess: "csma" is half-duplex plus listen-before-talk over peers in range — what BLE and Wi-Fi both do at the link layer — with no contention window, no random backoff and no RTS/CTS. Three seeds, receiver model on in every row:

It does exactly what it was invented to do — self-deafness all but disappears (693 → 7), and a unit test pins the smallest case: two nodes transmitting at each other produce one deaf loss and one retry blind, and zero of each when they listen, paid for with 573 ms of waiting. A second test pins what it cannot do: in a three-node line the two ends are out of each other's range but both in range of the middle, and the collision survives carrier sense exactly as it survives blind transmission — the hidden terminal, which is what RTS/CTS exists for. And it is still the pessimistic arm, because waiting is not free: the bounded queue overflows harder (1,441 → 2,995 drops) while the layers above keep generating packets.

The obvious response to "the queue overflows" is a bigger queue, and it is monotonic in the wrong direction in both arms: at depth 128 the deferred air time totals seven hours across three 60-node runs, and the mesh spends its whole life waiting for a radio horizon that keeps moving. That is bufferbloat with a discrete-event clock, and it is the same runaway the first implementation of the channel model hit by accident (§5's note below). The mechanism that would change the verdict is backpressure: a real stack defers and stops generating work, while this engine only defers — its MPR fan-out, retry timers and transfer window keep producing packets whatever the radio is doing.

The contention window carrier sense was missing — and why it is still not the fix

§12.10 diagnosed carrier sense as “waiting is not free”. There is a second thing wrong and it is structural: every node deferring on a busy medium is released at the same instant the medium clears, because that instant is what it was scheduled against. They all find it free together and transmit together, so listening synchronises the herd instead of dispersing it. channelBackoff: "binary" adds the standard answer — draw a slot count from the current window, wait it out, double the window after a collision, reset it after a clean transmission. Consulted only under csma; ships off, so it consumes no random numbers and the golden vectors are byte-identical.

It costs 8-16% of file time to find out.

The reason it cannot work is in the columns nobody reads first. Across every arm, queue drops run 1994-3466 while receiver collisions run 273-394: about eight packets shed at a full transmit queue for every one lost to a collision. Under carrier sense the dominant loss is buffering, not collision — and a contention window makes nodes wait longer, feeding the mechanism that is actually losing the packets. Widening the window from 2 to 64 slots does cut collisions (370 → 273) and cuts delivery anyway. That revises §12.10’s own diagnosis: what is missing is not a wider window in front of the queue but flow control above it. At three nodes in a triangle the same result appears in miniature — the backoff fires (8 draws), turns 1250 ms of deferral into 2054 ms, and the collision count does not move. halfDuplex + backoff is inert by construction and asserted so (0.9096, 0 draws, 3.28 min, identical to off).

§12.10 ended by naming backpressure as the missing half of the radio model, and the first measurement of mediaBackpressure in a mesh came out null, so this report explained it with an argument about topology: the queue that overflows belongs to a relay, which several senders feed at once, so throttling one source leaves it exactly as full. The argument was never measured, and measuring it contradicts it. With no media in flight the shedding really is a relay’s, 80–83%. With a file in flight it inverts: 82–85% of the shed packets are dropped by the node that originated them, and 79–82% of what it sheds is transfer chunks. The premise was measured on the wrong arm.

The corrected mechanism cuts drops in both arms and moves delivery in opposite directions: +1.7 points under carrier sense, −2.8 under plain half-duplex, and neither reaches the ≥0.90 gate. Under halfDuplex it buys a faster file with text delivery, which is the wrong way round for an acceptance box about text; under csma it buys delivery with a p95 that nearly doubles.

The second thing the attribution settles is the cheap alternative to flow control. Rerouting round a saturated relay needs somewhere to reroute to — a peer in range whose own route to the destination is no longer than the one already chosen — and one existed for 3–9% of relay sheds. This topology does not have the path diversity, so congestion-aware forwarding is closed without being built. What is left being shed after the corrected gate is not the transfer’s to give up: 70% of it is still shed at a source and 66% is still chunks, because a window can ration a transfer’s share of a radio but cannot create slots. The remaining mechanism is a radio that can refuse the hand-over rather than shed it — which is what §12.10, §12.12, §12.13 and §12.14 each independently arrive at. docs/08 §12.12–12.14.

The defect the channel model found in the retry timer

Every radio number above was measured twice. The first pass ran with a retry timer that started when a message was handed to the radio rather than when it went on air. Under channelAccess: "free" those are the same instant, so nothing in §1–§11 of docs/08 and no golden vector was affected — but under a queued radio a packet can wait hundreds of milliseconds, so the timer expired before the packet had been transmitted and retransmitted it into the same saturated queue. Five members, 20 messages, one member out of range for the whole conversation. A beacon is 156 characters whatever the group has said - 16 XOR-folded bucket hashes - so one beacon is one 256 B BLE packet, and merging is a set union, so beacons may be lost, duplicated or reordered without the protocol caring. Idle cost is 31 beacons/min and 81 B/s.

The boundary is the finding: beacons go to co-members in range, so what converges is the membership's peer graph. A spread membership recovers the returning member completely and still never converges, because two members who cannot hear each other and have no chain between them can never compare digests. Reach is the scoped flood's job; repair only fixes the last hop of a loss.

7. Store-carry-forward: the two defects a test found (docs/06 §20)

skills/04 specifies the DTN layer in four rules and three acceptance boxes. The queue, the 500-message cap, the seven-day expiry and the spray limit were all implemented as written - and the first box, "A enqueues for C (C offline); B meets A then later C → C receives", did not pass. Two independent defects, both found by writing the test:

  • Sprayed copies reused the source retries' attempt numbers. A bundle is parked after the originator's two retries have used attempts 1 and 2; the spray numbered its copies from 1, and since relays dedupe on id#attempt, every relay that had heard the retries threw away the first two of three copies. The whole spray budget was spent on copies nobody forwarded.
  • A carried bundle with no route was deleted by flood suppression. With the destination offline the route lookup fails, the copy falls into the flood branch, and a NORMAL node MPR-suppresses it - the log reads "heard relays cover all my neighbours", where the relays it heard were the originator's own transmissions. Suppression exists to delete duplicate copies of a flood; a bundle with nowhere to go is not a duplicate, it is the message.

9. Defects found, and what they cost

#Defect and consequenceEvidence
1tq tracked per destination instead of per (originator, via) link
Every route looked as good as the best neighbour advertising it. Direct-neighbour tq 0.107 -> 0.935, usable routes 29.7% -> 86.8%.
docs/06 §7
2The flood-all baseline was not actually naive
It reused the route table, so the overhead ratio compared two routing policies. Rewritten as epidemic flooding, and a route-flood arm added so suppression can be isolated from routing.
docs/06 §6
3A node relayed copies of its own message
The originator treated its own broadcast as fresh traffic. Counting it as a duplicate cut relay transmissions 18.3% in a warm campus (3,009 -> 2,458) and moved the golden vectors, which were regenerated.
docs/06 §12
4The MPR fallback went quadratic at density
At 200 nodes a beacon advertises at most 30 of ~179 heard peers, so coverage becomes unanswerable and the fallback fires for every peer: one 3-hop delivery cost 7,038 transmissions. Bounded to six sector-spread peers: -67% at 50 nodes, -86% at 100, -76% at 200.
docs/06 §15
5The BLE payload ceiling was assumed at 241 characters
The real packet model is ceil((95 + body)/256) x 256, so the ceiling is 930 characters - 3.9x more text per BLE packet than the engine had been assuming.
docs/07 §4
6A media transfer could hang forever
A chunk whose retries run out is carried in an OppQueue and its logical id is never seen again, so the sliding window never advances. A stall watchdog re-sends what went on air and never arrived; skills/05's 'resume after mid-transfer disconnect' only passes because of it.
docs/08 §6
7Media retried on the text plane's 4 s timer
Four seconds is eight 500 ms voice frames, so one lost frame left an 8.53 s hole in the audio. A 600 ms media timer cut the worst gap to 1.21 s and underruns from 16 to 7 over three seeds.
docs/08 §7
8Push-to-talk frames left one frame before they were recorded
The first model scheduled frame i at i x 500 ms, which made a 3 s note fully received 400 ms before release - a negative play delay. Frames cover [i, i+1) x 500 ms, so they leave at the end of that window; the last one departs at release.
docs/08 §5.1
9A voice blob was transmitted before it existed
The non-streaming arm queued the whole note at capture start, which flattered it against streaming. Both modes now release at now + seconds.
docs/08 §5.1
10A bounded anti-entropy reply stalled at the cap
Replying with the first 32 entries of the differing buckets re-offered the same head of the same sorted list every round, so a 20-message absence converged to 8 and stopped. The request now lists the ids the requester already holds.
docs/06 §17.2
11Digest beacons were counted as 1:1 traffic
They inflated sentUnicast and made deliveryRatio a statement about how often a group repaired itself. Counted apart as sentSync and excluded from the ratio, the chat log and the dashboard's message list.
docs/06 §17.6
12A 10 MB file rendered as 160 empty chat bubbles
The chat panel drew one bubble per sent record. Chunks are now filtered out and rendered as one progress row per transfer, with the radio it is riding and the window stalls it has taken.
docs/08 §3.1
13Sprayed DTN copies reused the source retries' attempt numbers
A bundle is parked after the originator's two retries have used attempts 1 and 2, and the spray numbered its copies from 1 - so every relay that had heard the retries deduped the first two copies on id#attempt and the whole spray budget was spent on copies nobody forwarded. skills/04's carry test could not pass.
docs/06 §20.1
14A carried bundle with no route was deleted by MPR suppression
With the destination offline the carrier's route lookup fails, the copy falls into the flood branch, and a NORMAL node suppresses it - the log reads 'heard relays cover all my neighbours', where the relays it heard were the originator's own transmissions. Suppression deletes duplicate copies of a flood; a bundle with nowhere to go is the message. Both fixes together: crowd delivery 0.867 -> 0.947 over six seeds.
docs/06 §20.1
15The OppQueue cap evicted by age alone
'Keep newest 500 (LRU by priority)' was implemented as 'drop the oldest', which could throw away a fresh bundle to keep one that had already sprayed its three copies and been demoted. Priority now ranks before age.
docs/06 §20.3

10. Levers measured and deliberately not shipped

LeverWhy not
Faster OGM beacons in the crowd
1 s intervals looked like +2.6 points of within-5-hop delivery on three seeds and measured -1.1 on six. The sign flipped, not just the magnitude.
docs/06 §16.4
More source retries (4 instead of 2)
Delivery 91.7% -> 93.2% in the crowd, at a p95 of 10.9 s - buying 1.5 points with a latency budget the spec does not have.
docs/06 §16
A contention window for carrier sense
Built, measured, rejected: ±1.1 points on delivery and both csma arms still ~14 short of the gate. With a file in flight the best six-seed mean clears it (0.9040 against 0.8672) while its spread widens, so it does not hold per-run. The decomposition is the finding — under carrier sense the losses are queue drops (1994-3466) rather than collisions (273-394), so the lever aimed at collisions cannot be the fix. docs/08 §12.13
docs/08 §12.13
A signal-strength capture effect at the receiver
Built, measured, rejected: the mechanism is exactly monotone in its own knob — takeovers fall 167, 100, 58, 16, 2 across margins of 0, 3, 6, 12 and 20 dB — and delivery does not move with it, a flat band from 0.8616 to 0.8898 across an eighty-fold change in how often it fires. Six seeds with a file in flight: csma 0.8672, which is first-packet-wins to four decimals, and halfDuplex 0.8983 against first’s 0.9209, so under plain half-duplex it is 2.3 points worse than the pessimistic model it was meant to improve on. A takeover is not a rescue: it moves the loss from the newcomer to the incumbent, one packet is lost either way, and the incumbent now has a retry to do. The first measurement of this read 0.9266 and appeared to clear the gate; the whole of that was the incumbent’s link being booked as a success before its fate was known, and booking the outcome at arrival instead removed it. docs/08 §12.15
docs/08 §12.15
Adaptive media retry timers
Both RFC 6298's srtt + 4 rttvar and a min-RTT rule were implemented and measured; both lose to a fixed 600 ms (file at 2 hops: 31.96 s and 28.38 s against 24.09 s). For a deadline-driven stream an early duplicate beats a correctly-sized timeout. Left in the code, off by default.
docs/08 §7.1
Deeper transfer windows
Window 16 is 15.5x faster than window 1 at one hop — on an infinitely wide radio. On the one radio a phone has the same sweep is worth 1.25x, and depth starts dropping chunks, because there is one transmitter to fill and the parallelism was never there. Ships at 4 under free; window 1 is the evidence-backed setting under halfDuplex, where it recovers 1.1 of the 1.7 points of delivery a file costs and a third of the p95, for 46% more transfer time.
docs/08 §4, §12.2, §12.5
Promoting acknowledgements or voice in the transmit queue
Both implemented (channelPriority) and both off. Neither is a win: promoting ACKs buys text 1.1 points of delivery and 158 ms of p95 and charges the file 14% for it, and promoting voice on top is the best arm for a note competing with a file (49.26 s of play delay against 1.33 min) while costing 1.7 points of text delivery instead. No queue order rescues push-to-talk at three hops, because the limit is the note's own air time and no ordering creates air time.
docs/08 §12.8
Carrier sense
Implemented as channelAccess: "csma" and off. It removes almost every collision a node can hear (self-deafness 693 → 7) and still costs delivery under load (0.9096 → 0.8757 with a file in flight) and fails both acceptance gates (0.7389-0.7944 delivery, 78-80% overhead), because waiting is not free in an engine whose layers above keep generating packets. Deepening the queue is monotonic in the wrong direction: 0.7062 delivery and 429 minutes of deferred air time at depth 128. The missing mechanism is backpressure.
docs/08 §12.10
Assuming coverage from density
A rule that treated 'heard many SUPERs' as 'covered' measured 95.0% delivery against 98.3% for the sector-bounded fallback.
docs/06 §15
Preferring SUPER as bundle carrier (skills/04)
Implemented and off by default. It measured +4.5 delivery points in the crowd *before* the two DTN defects were fixed and -3.5 points after them, with parked bundles growing 39 -> 63 per run: spraying toward SUPERs duplicates work into the busiest queues. The rule's intuition is about who should hold a bundle, not who should be handed extra copies.
docs/06 §20.4
Gossiping ACKs so relays can prune bundles (skills/04)
Implemented exactly as written - the bundle is acknowledged to the carrier that handed it over, and any node hearing the ACK drops its copy - and it pruned zero bundles in four worlds over six seeds, because the ACK is a unicast along the reverse path. Kept because it is free; a prune flood is not worth adding until a deployment shows cap pressure (worst measured: 97 of 500 slots).
docs/06 §20.5
Relaying history digests across the mesh
It would converge a spread membership and would cost a scoped flood every interval, forever, to repair traffic that mostly arrived. Not built; the peer-graph boundary is documented instead.
docs/06 §17.4
Normalising peer count against the observed maximum
Implemented as peerNorm: "observed" and rejected on measurement. The predicted trap was §15's density rule from the other side; the real problem is that an election is a ranking and this is a level change. The most open node's score rises 0.05-0.19 in all four worlds and its rank inside its own candidacy list does not move once (7 of 13 on campus, 2 of 4 in the village, 12 of 25 in the crowd), so it stays NORMAL in three worlds out of four either way. What it does move is the promote gate and the size of the elected set (village 2 → 3 SUPERs, crowd 13 → 11), and it spends skills/02's margin: over six campus seeds one lands exactly on the 15% floor, for under half a point of delivery.
docs/06 §18.4
Preferring a community relay as MPR or bundle carrier
Both places the protocol chooses who does the work, both measured, both off. As an MPR tie-break it wins the tie it was written for and moves 0.6 of 100 slots in a crowd, with every metric identical to the digit across three seeds — who transmits is decided per packet from what was heard, not from the MPR label. As a bundle carrier it fires 40-51 times a run and grows parked bundles 27-55% for at most 0.7 extra opportunistic deliveries: a node that does not move is the best place to leave a bundle and the worst place to carry one.
docs/06 §19.5
Cap-aware community-relay promotion
Implemented in both proposed forms (communityRelayQuota: reserve part of the cap, or exempt tablets from it) and rejected on three seeds. The diagnosis decides it: a tablet that fails to be elected lost to other tablets — six better-scoring community relays against zero phones on seed 42 — so a reservation cannot help it, and the proximity guard that stops the others is docs/03 §5 working as written. Exemption does elect all six and lifts delivery 1.7 points, but it raises everybody else's transmissions 12% and puts the SUPER ratio at 25.0%, the ceiling of skills/02's band. Reserving half the cap reserves nothing: those slots were already the tablets', so what it actually does is hand one to a phone.
docs/06 §19.4
Sleeping stabilises routing
Falsified: the duty cycle is delivery-neutral (+0.8/+0.5 points) and its value is battery, not stability.
docs/06 §7
Raising data TTL for the crowd's 5-hop tail
The tail decomposes into 2.2% of paths that are 6+ hops, 1.8% with no path at all, 5.8% lost or expired, and 5.8% in-flight churn - only one of which a TTL would touch.
docs/06 §16
Two methodological rules came out of this and are now written into the evidence document rather than remembered: an A/B baseline must not share the mechanism under test, and a parameter change proposed on three seeds of a 60-node mobile world needs six before anybody believes it - the OGM-interval lever changed sign between the two.

14. What remains

Twenty-eight roadmap items are still open, and every one of them is app or platform work rather than protocol work: the BLE GATT hello and iOS beacon, permissions flows, the foreground service, SQLite, WifiP2pManager and MultipeerConnectivity, the Opus codec, QR pairing, distribution, legal.

That sentence said twenty-nine, and the twenty-ninth was wrong. Phase 1 item 15, the MeshTransport {advertise(), scan(), connect(), send()} abstraction, carried a note saying the interface itself was app work. It is not: the interface is pure Dart with no dependency beyond the SDK, and the decisions inside it — what a 12-byte beacon contains and in which byte order, which radio a payload rides and at what size, when a connection is refused rather than attempted, and what a scan is permitted to report — are testable without a phone. skills/01 had already specified all six verbs, so the seam is built to that signature with SimulatedMeshTransport as a reference implementation and a test double, and it lifts into the mono-repo's core/ unchanged when item 13 creates it. What is still app work is the four concrete implementations beneath it. The useful part of the correction is not the item count: it is that “needs hardware” had been used to close a category rather than to check it, and one member of the category was checkable.

Two of its rules are load-bearing rather than plumbing. connect refuses a peer the local scan has not reported even when that peer exists in the world, and scan reads the device's own scan cycle — stale while a NORMAL node sleeps — rather than the geometry. An implementation that reaches an unheard peer hands the app an oracle, and an app built on an oracle passes every test in this repository and then fails on a phone in a pocket. A test moves a peer into range mid-run and asserts the two disagree at that instant and agree again later, so the invariant cannot pass vacuously. And the seam consumes no random numbers: a world driven through it is identical to the same world driven by hand, delivery for delivery, so the cross-language golden vectors hold.

On the simulation side five things remain named in the evidence documents as the next lever to pull, and four of the five are now answered: the community-relay preference and observed-maximum normalisation were both built, measured and rejected; hop-by-hop flow control was built, found broken in three places, corrected and measured as a trade rather than a rescue (§5); the voice-priority policy is the channelPriority: "class" arm, measured in §5 and not shipped because promoting voice does not pay; and the signal-strength capture effect is built, measured and rejected in §10 — RTS/CTS along with it, since a receiver that can be taken over by a stronger packet is the mechanism RTS/CTS exists to protect, and neither changes how many packets are lost. All five are now answered, and none of them ships. Twelve levers were built and measured while writing this report — two channel-access models, two receiver models, two queue disciplines, a contention window, sender-side backpressure, a community-relay cap and preference, a DTN carrier preference, and observed-maximum peer normalisation — and every one of them defaults to the behaviour the acceptance gates were met under, which is why the golden vectors never moved. Their results are in §5 and §8, and the two that changed a published conclusion are called out where they were measured.

  • Preference rather than promotion for community relays - the cap-aware promotion §19.2 asked for is built, measured and rejected (§19.4): a tablet that is not elected lost to other tablets, so the useful question is whether a plugged node should be preferred as an MPR and as a bundle carrier, where the work actually is.
  • Rank, not score, in the field record - observed-maximum normalisation is built, measured and rejected (§18.4): the atrium phone loses on rank among the candidates it can hear, which a score threshold hides. docs/09 §5 should record that column.
  • Hop-by-hop flow control - the sender-side version is built, was found broken in three places, and is now corrected (mediaBackpressure): on one radio with nobody else on it it cuts a 1 MB transfer's queue drops from 25 to 5, its retransmissions from 6 to 0 and its stalls from 1 to 0, and the file arrives in 6.04 s instead of 9.37 s. In a mesh it is a trade rather than a win — +1.7 points of delivery under carrier sense, −2.8 under plain half-duplex, and neither reaches the gate. The version that would fix the mesh is a radio that can refuse a hand-over rather than shed it, with a layer above that holds the packet instead of counting it lost, which nothing in this stack has (docs/08 §12.12–12.14). Three measurements make this the load-bearing gap rather than one of several: under carrier sense the losses are queue drops outnumbering collisions about eight to one (§12.13); with a file in flight 82–85% of the shedding is the source's own, not a relay's, which withdraws the topological argument this list used to carry (§12.14); and the cheap alternative — rerouting round a congested relay — is unavailable, because an equal-cost alternative next hop existed for only 3–9% of relay sheds.
  • A capture effect that compares signal strengths, and RTS/CTS - the receiver model loses the later of two packets whatever their levels, and the hidden terminal is provably unfixable without RTS/CTS (a test pins it). Both are named with their direction in docs/08 §12.9-12.10. The third omission that list carried is now closed: two nodes that defer for each other were separated only by event ordering, so a contention window was built — and measured, and rejected (§5 above, docs/08 §12.13). It is the first radio lever to move the carrier-sense mean toward the gate at all, and it does not hold per-seed.
  • Voice priority in the transmit queue - at three hops a streamed note cannot keep up with its own capture clock on one radio, so either streaming is capped at two hops and longer notes are sent as blobs, or voice jumps the queue. Not implemented; it is the next thing to measure (docs/08 §12.3).
